What does PDF to scanned PDF conversion do?

A PDF to scanned PDF conversion takes each page of a digital PDF, draws it onto a canvas at a chosen pixel density, and stores the result as a picture inside a new PDF. The output looks the same, but every word, line and logo is now part of one image per page.

A real scanner does the same thing with paper instead of a digital page. The U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California describes the result plainly: "Scanned text is a photograph of a printed page produced either by a digital camera or scanner." Once a page is a photograph, selecting, copying or searching its words no longer works until someone runs optical character recognition (OCR) on it.

Two related terms get mixed up. To flatten a PDF in the strict sense means merging interactive layers (form fields, comments, stamps) into the page content, while text can stay selectable. To rasterize a PDF means turning everything, text included, into pixels. Converting a PDF to a scanned PDF is the second, stronger operation.

There are two flavors of result. A clean image-only PDF keeps the page crisp and straight. A scan-look PDF adds a slight tilt, soft focus, noise and a paper tint. Our free PDF to scanned PDF tool produces both from the same controls. For the background on what counts as a scanned file, see what is a scanned PDF.

What changes when a PDF becomes a scanned PDF?

When a PDF becomes a scanned PDF, the visible page stays the same while everything machine-readable on the page is replaced by pixels. Text, fonts, links, form fields and annotations stop being separate objects. File size usually grows, and search, copy and screen reading stop working until OCR is applied.

Which resolution keeps a scanned PDF readable?

A scanned PDF stays readable when each letter gets enough pixels. Our tool renders PDF pages at 72 dpi multiplied by the Resolution setting, so 1× is about 72 dpi, 2× about 144 dpi and 3× about 216 dpi. Real archival scanning targets 200 to 300 ppi, so 3× suits small print.

The 72 comes from how PDF measures pages. PDF and PostScript geometry is built on 72 points per inch, so a US Letter page is 612 × 792 points. Rendering it at 1× gives 612 × 792 pixels, at 2× gives 1,224 × 1,584 pixels, and at 3× gives 1,836 × 2,376 pixels. Pixel count grows with the square of the scale.

How small print behaves at each scanned PDF resolution

A simple calculation shows why small text needs more pixels. An 8-point footnote is 8/72 of an inch tall as a font size. At 1× that is about 8 pixels for the whole letter height including ascenders and descenders, which blurs lowercase "e" and "c" together. At 2× it becomes about 16 pixels, and at 3× about 24 pixels, which reads comfortably even after light blur and JPEG compression.

For comparison, the U.S. National Archives requires at least 300 ppi for digitized modern textual records and 400 ppi for photographs and fine-detail records under 36 CFR 1236 Subpart E. FADGI rates unbound documents in star levels from 200 ppi (one star) through 250 and 300 up to 400 ppi (four stars) in its 2023 technical guidelines. Those targets protect paper originals.

A PDF to scanned PDF conversion starts from a perfect digital page, not wrinkled paper, so it does not need archival density to look sharp. Our experience is that 2× handles normal 10–12 point text, and 3× (216 dpi) sits just above the FADGI one-star level for fine print.

NARA adds a useful caution in the same regulation: "The agency cannot rely solely on equipment specifications, such as scanner ppi settings or camera sensor megapixels, to ensure digital image quality." In practice, judge the preview, not the number.

Gray, color or bitonal: which keeps the file small?

Bitonal (pure black and white) files are the smallest, grayscale sits in the middle, and color is the largest at the same resolution. For a PDF to scanned PDF conversion, grayscale is the practical default: it keeps anti-aliased letter edges smooth and stays far smaller than color. Use color only when color carries information.

The strongest real-world numbers come from the Northern District of California, which scanned the same 317-page document two ways. As a bitonal file at 300 ppi it weighed 23.86 MB and stayed legible; in grayscale at 400 ppi it grew to 374.47 MB. That is roughly fifteen times larger for the same pages, driven by both the color depth and the higher resolution.

Scanned PDF file size limits you may hit

Upload caps make the color choice practical rather than cosmetic. The Western District of Washington sets a 100 MB per-document limit and asks for 300 dpi black-and-white scans unless color is integral. The District of Minnesota caps each exhibit at 35 MB in its criminal ECF procedures. USCIS limits each uploaded evidence file to 12 MB.

Quick size ruleStart at 2× and Gray. If the downloaded file is too large for a portal, drop to 1.5× before switching anything else, because halving resolution steps cuts pixels far more than any other setting. If it is still too large, split the PDF and convert the parts separately.

When is a scanned PDF required or refused?

A scanned PDF is sometimes requested (image-only uploads, flattened final copies) but often refused where text search matters. Federal courts and accessibility rules usually prefer text-searchable PDFs, so check the recipient's rules before you convert a PDF to scanned PDF, and keep the original file.

Courts are the clearest example. The federal judiciary's FAQ states that "CM/ECF systems are designed to accept only documents in PDF format," but the details differ by district. The Southern District of Florida is strict: "pleadings/main documents filed electronically in CM/ECF must be in text searchable PDF format", and exhibits should be searchable whenever possible.

Other districts express a preference rather than a ban. The District of Massachusetts writes that "Documents converted to PDF, rather than scanned, are preferred for filing in CM/ECF." The District of Minnesota says "Text-searchable scanned PDF documents are preferred, but not required." The Eastern District of Oklahoma advises creating PDF/A files directly from a word processor instead of printing and scanning, to keep files small and searchable.

Accessibility is the second reason for refusal. The W3C's WCAG technique PDF7 states: "A document that consists of scanned images of text is inherently inaccessible because the content of the document is images, not searchable text." Public-facing documents from agencies, universities and many companies must meet WCAG, so an image-only file is the wrong format for them unless OCR is added.

Check the rules, and use the scan look honestlyIf a court, agency or employer requires text-searchable PDFs, file the original digital PDF, or run OCR on the scanned version afterwards so a hidden text layer returns. Our tool does not perform OCR. Also remember that a scan look changes appearance, not authenticity: do not use it to alter or forge official documents, IDs or signatures that are not yours.

Adding a text layer back with OCR

If you need both the flattened appearance and searchability, convert first and then run OCR in desktop PDF software that offers a "recognize text" or "make searchable" step. OCR places invisible text behind the page image, so the file looks scanned while Ctrl+F works again. Proofread important documents, because OCR estimates the letters.

How do you check the result is a scanned PDF?

Check a converted file by trying to select text, searching for a word, and zooming into the smallest print. A true scanned PDF lets you select nothing but whole-page images, finds no words with Ctrl+F, and still reads cleanly at 200 percent zoom. Also confirm the file size and page count.

Cleveland State University's accessibility guide describes the quickest test: "If the word becomes highlighted on the page, then it has searchable text as opposed to being a scanned image of text." After a proper conversion nothing highlights, and dragging the cursor selects the page image instead of words.

Five-point check before you send1. Text cannot be selected or copied. 2. Ctrl+F finds nothing. 3. The smallest print reads at 200 percent zoom. 4. Page count and order match the original. 5. File size fits the recipient's upload limit, and document properties show only what you intended.

How do you fix PDF to scanned PDF problems?

Most PDF to scanned PDF problems come from four settings: resolution too low (blurry text), resolution or color too high (huge file), a non-PDF source (wrong page size), and rotation left on (tilted pages). Each has a direct fix in the tool, listed below with the setting to change first.

Blurry or broken text in the scanned PDF

Raise Resolution to 2.5× or 3×, then lower Blur to 0.1–0.2 or 0 for a clean flatten. If thin gray text still fades, raise Contrast to about 1.2. Blur and noise are applied after rendering, so at 1× even a small blur value can make 9-point text unreadable.

The scanned PDF file is huge

Switch Colorspace to Gray if the document is text, then step Resolution down from 3× to 2× or 1.5×. Noise also costs space, because random speckles are hard for JPEG to compress, so keep Noise at 0.1 or lower on long documents. For very long files, split them and convert in parts.

Wrong page size after converting to scanned

PDF pages keep their original size and layout. If your output shows A4 pages with plain Helvetica text, the source was a DOCX, spreadsheet, TXT or HTML file, which the tool re-typesets as text on A4. Export the original to PDF from its own app first, then convert that PDF.

Pages are tilted or corners are cut off

Set Rotate and Rotate variance to 0 for a straight page. The defaults (Rotate 1°) are there for realism, but a legal filing or a form headed for automated processing should usually be straight. If you want tilt, keep it under 2° so corners stay inside the page.

Signature or stamp looks pixelated

Signature lines are thin and suffer first at low resolution. Use 2.5× or 3×, keep Blur at or below 0.3, and if you place a stamp or signature image through the extras, start from a high-resolution PNG. Only place signatures and stamps you are authorized to use.

We built the Resolution control around the PDF's own 72-point grid so the numbers stay honest: 2× is 144 dpi, 3× is 216 dpi, nothing hidden. That is why the defaults are 2× in Gray: sharp enough for normal body text, and far smaller than a color render of the same page. PDF to scanned PDF: frequently asked questions

These PDF to scanned PDF answers cover the questions people ask most after converting: what happens to hidden text and metadata, whether the change can be undone, how OCR fits in, which settings to pick, and whether the tool works on phones. Each answer reflects what the tool actually does.

Does converting a PDF to a scanned PDF remove hidden text?

Yes, from the page content. The new pages are JPEG images of what the renderer draws, so there is no text layer left to copy, including white text or words hidden behind a black box. Treat this as a side effect, and still inspect the preview when a redaction matters.

What happens to the metadata of the original PDF?

The tool writes a new PDF and lets you set its title, author, subject, keywords, producer and creator in the extras panel. Fill those fields in the way you want them before downloading, then check the document properties of the result in your PDF viewer.

Can a scanned PDF be converted back to an editable PDF?

Not exactly. The original text layer, fonts, links and form fields are gone. OCR can recognize the letters and add a new searchable layer, and some software can rebuild editable text from it, but formatting and accuracy will not match the original. Keep the original file.

Can I run OCR on the scanned PDF afterwards?

Yes. Any OCR step in desktop PDF software will treat the output like a normal scan. Higher resolution and low noise give better recognition, so use 2× or 3× with Noise near 0 if you plan to OCR the file.

What is the difference between "flatten PDF" and "PDF to scanned"?

Flattening in the narrow sense merges form fields and annotations into the page but may keep text selectable. Converting a PDF to scanned turns the entire page, text included, into an image. The second is stronger and is what our tool does.

What resolution should I choose to convert a PDF to scanned PDF?

Use 2× (about 144 dpi) for normal documents and 3× (about 216 dpi) for fine print, dense tables or signatures. Drop to 1.5× only when the file must be very small and the text is 12 points or larger.

Why is my scanned PDF so much bigger than the original?

A text PDF stores letters as compact instructions; a scanned PDF stores every pixel of every page. Gray instead of Color, a lower Resolution and less Noise all reduce size. Courts report the same effect: one 317-page test file ranged from 23.86 MB to 374.47 MB depending on color mode and resolution.

Will a court accept a PDF converted to a scanned PDF?

It depends on the court. Some districts require text-searchable main documents, others only prefer them. Read the local CM/ECF rules first; when in doubt, file the original digital PDF or add OCR to the scanned version.

Does the PDF to scanned PDF tool work on a phone?

Yes. The website runs in mobile browsers, processes files locally on the phone and downloads the scanned PDF to your files. Large documents at 3× take longer and use more memory on phones, so 2× is the safer choice there.
