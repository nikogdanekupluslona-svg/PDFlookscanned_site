# Danish (da) glossary: pdflookscanned.com

Market: Denmark. Locale `da_DK`. URL path `/da/`. Every Danish page (UI, chrome, home guide, the 7 articles) uses
these terms, so the site reads as one voice. Read `seo-workspace/translations/TRANSLATOR-GUIDE.md` first; this file
adds only the Danish-specific decisions.

## 1. Address form and style

- **Address the reader as "du"** (du / dig / din / dit / dine). Never "De". Modern Danish tool sites and software
  are informal. Use "man" only for general statements ("Man får en PDF til at se scannet ud, når …").
- **Short, plain sentences.** Danish readers skim. Use one main clause and at most one subordinate clause, and
  keep the answer first. Avoid English calques: write "Det tager under et minut", not "Det tager mindre end et minut at gøre".
- **Imperatives without accent** (both are correct in Danish; we pick one): Konverter, Eksporter, Juster,
  Generer, Vælg, Træk, Klik, Download, Upload, Åbn, Gem, Tjek.
- **Comma:** grammatical comma WITH "startkomma", i.e. a comma before every subordinate clause
  ("Tjek, at teksten kan læses", "den fane, du arbejder i", "Når den er indlæst, virker den offline").
  Put a comma before "og" when it joins two full imperative clauses ("Åbn værktøjet, træk en PDF ind, og download.").
  Be consistent across the page.
- **Capitals:** only the first word of headings and titles is capitalized ("Sådan får du en PDF til at se scannet
  ud"), plus proper names and abbreviations. After a colon, write lowercase unless a full sentence follows
  (Danish rule: capital after a colon when a complete main clause follows, e.g. in list intros "Ja. …").
- **Quotes:** Danish guillemets »…« (e.g. »scannet kopi«). Translated quotes from English sources also use »…«,
  followed by the attribution and the unchanged link.
- **Dashes:** en dash with spaces for parentheses/asides " – "; en dash without spaces in ranges "0,2–0,4",
  "20–60 sekunder". Never an em dash.
- **Abbreviations and file types with inflection:** apostrophe before the ending: PDF'en, PDF'er, PDF'erne,
  JPG'en, ZIP-filen (compound with hyphen). "PDF" is common gender: en PDF, den scannede PDF.
  Compounds with abbreviations take a hyphen: PDF-fil, PDF-side, PDF-metadata, ZIP-arkiv, JPEG-komprimering,
  OCR-lag, Word-fil, Excel-ark, Chrome-udvidelse, HR-afdeling.
- **Loanwords:** "look" is normal Danish ("et scannet look", "vintage-look"). "Scanne" is conjugated in Danish:
  at scanne, scanner, scannede, har scannet, en scanning, scanninger, en scannet PDF, scannede PDF'er.
- **Tone:** factual, friendly, no hype ("revolutionerende", "fantastisk" are banned just like in English).

## 8. Things to avoid in Danish text

- No competitor or third-party product names (see `seo-workspace/qa.py` FORBIDDEN): describe them generically.
  Do not mention a scanning feature of a cloud-storage app. Do not write "lookscanned".
- Do not claim creases, folds, stains, edge shadows, perspective warp or OCR as tool features.
- Do not translate "Make PDF look scanned" or "Microsoft Print to PDF".
- Do not use English UI labels in running text when a Danish label exists (write "Generer scannet PDF",
  not "Generate Scanned PDF").
